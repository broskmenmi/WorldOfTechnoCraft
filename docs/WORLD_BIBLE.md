# WORLD OF TECHNOCRAFT
## The Official World Bible — v1.0 ("The Demo Nobody Asked For, Finished Anyway")

---

## 1. TONE MANIFESTO

**We are not laughing at the culture. We are the culture, laughing at ourselves in a club with no mirrors — which is why we've never actually seen ourselves, and why this game exists.**

### The Five Laws

1. **Play it completely straight.** The Red Alert rule: Tim Curry shouting "SPACE!" worked because he committed. Our world treats a 2-hour silent queue outside a power station as a sacred pilgrimage, a USB stick as a holy relic, and a man refusing you entry with "Heute leider nicht" as an oracle delivering fate. Nobody in-world knows they're funny. The mission briefings are delivered deadpan, direct to camera, addressing the player as **"Selector."**

2. **Punch sideways, never down.** Every faction roasts every other faction, and every faction is roastable — the snobbery graph is a circle, not a pyramid. We satirize *archetypes* (the messianic minimal purist, the sellout festival empire, the council noise officer), never real artists, real clubs by name, or real tragedies. The gabber horde is terrifying-then-endearing — hugging skinheads — never a fascism joke. Queer spaces are the *origin* of this culture and are treated as home turf, not punchline: the door exists to protect the room, and the game knows it even when the memes don't. Drugs are consequence-aware folklore (the friend melted into the couch, the 45-degree sway), never instructional, never glorified. Crowd disasters, police violence, and overdoses are off the joke menu entirely.

3. **The player's optimal strategy IS the joke.** Tropico law. You will find yourself strictening your door policy to farm prestige, charging €9 for water at your anarchist rave, and hiring a ghost producer for your "live" act — and the game will let you, and the game will remember, and your Sell-Out Meter will tick.

4. **Every joke must survive an insider's sniff test.** If a Berghain regular, a 1994 Castlemorton veteran, and a Rotterdam gabber can't each point at their own faction and say "that's me, unfortunately," cut the joke. Specificity is the whole product: it's not "a bouncer," it's a face-tattooed art photographer who was himself once rejected from a club in Sydney. It's not "loud music," it's music *"wholly or predominantly characterised by the emission of a succession of repetitive beats"* — an actual phrase from actual UK law, which we print on the loading screen like scripture.

5. **Failure must be audible.** Trombone Champ law. A trainwrecked transition plays badly-beatmatched audio to everyone in earshot. Losing sounds funny. Spectators matter.

### The Voice
Deadpan liturgical. The game speaks about techno the way the Vatican speaks about incense. Tooltips read like a fiscal court ruling that a nightclub is "high culture" (which really happened, and which we quote). The narrator has been awake since Saturday.

## 2. THE SIX PLAYABLE FACTIONS

### FACTION 1: THE 313 FOUNDATION *(Detroit Originators)*
**"We built this. Somewhere in Detroit."**

- **Personality:** The elder civilization. Masked, anonymous, Afrofuturist, morally undefeated. Machine music made by the people the machines abandoned. They communicate in runout-groove etchings and refuse all photography, all interviews, all major-label diplomacy. Their HQ location is listed on every map as "Somewhere in Detroit" — even on maps that are not Detroit.
- **Aesthetic:** Ski masks, black fatigues, sacred vinyl, mothership iconography, underwater Drexciyan sub-units, ruined auto plants humming back to life.
- **Units:** *Originators* (elite masked infantry, immune to Clout damage — you cannot cancel the anonymous), *Wax Couriers* (supply units carrying white-label vinyl; killing one drops a Rare Record item), *Hi-Tech Jazz Ensemble* (support squad projecting a strings-of-hope aura that heals morale in a wide radius), *The Submerged* (aquatic stealth units that surface behind enemy economies), *Radio Wizard* (three-deck siege platform, attacks three lanes at once).
- **Strengths:** Highest-prestige tech tree in the game; every other faction's units secretly respect them (enemy units within aura range suffer a small "they invented this" morale debuff). Immune to Sell-Out corruption. Vinyl doctrine mastery.
- **Weaknesses:** Slowest economy — they refuse sponsorship gold on principle. Small population cap. Their superweapon requires a working elevator containing exactly one funk unit and one robot unit.
- **Rivalry lines:**
  - vs. The Franchise: "You turned our future into a beach club. We turned your beach club into a target."
  - vs. Door-State: "One circle, Berlin. But remember who drew it."
  - vs. TikTok Swarm: "We wore masks so the music would be famous. You wear masks so *you* will be."

### FACTION 2: THE KONKRETE DOOR-STATE *(Berlin Klubnacht Theocracy)*
**"Heute leider nicht."**

- **Personality:** A monastic city-state occupying a fortress power plant, governed by an oracle bouncer whose judgments are final, unexplained, and canonically correct. Anti-commercial theocracy with the strictest bureaucracy in the game. Their national holiday lasts from Saturday midnight to Monday 08:00 and they consider Sunday afternoon the true session. A fiscal court has ruled them High Culture, and they have never recovered from the honor.
- **Aesthetic:** Expensive black, tiny sunglasses in a basement, 18-meter ceilings, camera stickers, no mirrors anywhere in the base (units literally cannot see their own health bars — an ally must inspect them).
- **Units:** *Monks of the Front Left* (elite dancers, eyes closed, economical two-step; gain power the longer they stand in one spot), *Door Oracles* (defensive structures-with-legs; filter enemy infiltrators via vibe-scan; 60–80% of ALL units approaching the base, including your own, are turned away — this is intended and is their core defense), *Awareness Team* (medics; also the only units allowed to talk on the dancefloor), *Stamp Clerks* (enable re-entry; lose your stamp, walk from the back of the map), *Klubnacht Veterans* (arrive Sunday at 15:00, fully rested, into battles other factions started Saturday).
- **Strengths:** Impregnable defense (the door is the wall); time-dilation aura on Sundays doubles all production; camera-sticker national policy makes their entire territory permanently fogged to enemy reconnaissance — you cannot screenshot their base, even in replays.
- **Weaknesses:** Their door rejects reinforcements they urgently need, including mercenaries they paid for, with no reason given and no appeal. Terminal self-seriousness: units refuse orders flagged as "cheesy." A €5 re-entry fee once triggered a civil war.
- **Rivalry lines:**
  - vs. Rotterdam Legion: "That is not music. That is a heart attack with distribution."
  - vs. The Franchise: "We have no VIP section. We ARE the VIP section. Of Europe. Of history. — No, you cannot come in."
  - vs. Free Tek Convoy: "We admire your ideology. Please queue anyway."

### FACTION 3: THE LEGION OF ROTTERDAM *(Gabber Horde)*
**"Hardcore is a state of the legs."**

- **Personality:** The melee horde. 190 BPM, shaved heads, Aussie tracksuits, Air Max, bomber jackets, and the warmest hearts on the battlefield — they will destroy your base and then hug you, because "gabber" literally means "mate." Founded on one grudge: a posher city sneered at them once, and their national anthem is still, functionally, *"Amsterdam, where's that then?"* Every enemy capital they attack is renamed "Amsterdam" in their unit barks regardless of what it actually is.
- **Aesthetic:** Identical unit skins (a canonical joke — the select-all box selects everyone, always), tracksuit armor, a temple called The Energiehal that they rebuild in every base and mourn preemptively.
- **Units:** *Hakkers* (fastest infantry in the game, moving via blindingly fast heel-toe micro; they physically cannot walk, only chop), *Terror Corps* (berserkers; damage scales with current BPM), *The Mate Wall* (a phalanx that is also a group hug), *Speedcore Bombardier* (artillery firing 200+ BPM distorted kicks; friendly units near the impact zone are HEALED by it), *Happy Hardcore Defectors* (cheap, cheerful, slightly embarrassing units your purist allies pretend not to see).
- **Strengths:** Speed. Overwhelming early aggression. Total immunity to fear, irony, and tempo debuffs. Highest unit loyalty stat in the game — Legion units never rout, they just hak in place until the situation improves.
- **Weaknesses:** Zero stealth capability (you can hear them from three map tiles). No unit can operate below 160 BPM — slow-tempo auras hard-counter them (see: New Beat Elders). Attrition wars drain them: the Legion metabolizes everything fast, including itself.
- **Rivalry lines:**
  - vs. Door-State: "128 BPM? Nice nap, mate. Wake me when the music starts."
  - vs. Gains Kingdom (hardstyle mercs): "You're our kids. Loud, shiny, and you stole the car."
  - vs. Anyone: "AMSTERDAM, WAAR LECH DAT DAN?" *(shouted at Detroit, London, Ibiza, and once at their own base by accident)*

### FACTION 4: THE FREE TEK CONVOY *(Squat/Free-Party Autonomous Zone)*
**"It's not illegal. It's unlicensed."**

- **Personality:** The nomad guerrilla faction. No fixed base — their capital is a convoy of trucks-as-homes, generators, and named sound systems that are legally people (in their legal system, which is a dog on a rope). Their founding trauma is a real law banning "repetitive beats," and their founding victory is a £4 million trial that ended in full acquittal — after the rigs were already seized. Everything meaningful about them involves the number 23.
- **Aesthetic:** Army surplus, patched hoodies, steel toecaps, diesel smell rendered as a visible particle effect, spiral iconography, 23s hidden in every texture (23 is also their max squad size, their tech tree has 23 nodes, and their theme is 23 minutes long).
- **Units:** *Rig Bearers* (mobile sound-system trucks = mobile production buildings; the wall of speakers has a name and a personality and grieves if destroyed), *Spiral Scouts* (find party sites; reveal map via phone-line treasure hunts), *Generator Saints* (power units; the whole faction runs on one diesel hum), *The Dog On A String* (free scout unit, unkillable, mandatory), *Acid Alchemists* (TB-303 artillery — a machine that failed at its intended job and became a god, which is also this faction's whole story).
- **Strengths:** No fixed base = nothing to siege; units cost zero gold (they cost Ideology, a resource only they have); can throw a party on ANY terrain, including terrain currently on fire; state repression makes them *stronger* (every enemy "raid" action spawns free reinforcements, because the crackdown is the recruitment poster).
- **Weaknesses:** Morale runs entirely on ideology — they instantly lose the game if they ever capture a bank, accept a sponsorship, or charge for a ticket. Chronically at war with their own splinter crews. Their toilets are a documented area-of-effect debuff.
- **Rivalry lines:**
  - vs. The Franchise: "You charge twenty euro for water. We charge nothing for everything. Who's the criminal?"
  - vs. Door-State: "A door policy? Mate, we don't even have a door."
  - vs. The 313 Foundation: *(respectful nod, no line — the only faction they don't call sellouts)*

### FACTION 5: THE FRANCHISE *(Ibiza Tech-House Babylon)*
**"It's not selling out if you were always for sale."**

- **Personality:** The economic superpower and the universal enemy — the one thing minimal monks, gabbers, crusties, and Detroit elders all agree on. White linen, veneers, table service, bottle sparklers as tribal torches. They are blissfully unaware of being everyone's villain, because they are richer and demonstrably happier than every other faction, which the purists can never, ever forgive. Their state religion is Vibes; their military doctrine is Networking.
- **Aesthetic:** Beach-club bases with VIP walls, gold everywhere, a fallen-Babylon glow. Every building is also a brand activation.
- **Units:** *Promo Reps* (basic infantry; attack by handing out wristbands), *VIP Hosts* (support units projecting a Table Service aura that converts gold into morale at ruinous exchange rates), *Sparkler Bearers* (flare units that reveal YOUR OWN position — intentionally; being seen is the point), *The Resident Superstar* (mercenary hero purchasable from ANY other faction's roster; arrives by private jet; costs "hundreds of thousands per night," reads as one line in your budget: *headliner economics*), *Content Teams* (phone-up units who deal no damage but steal enemy Vibe by filming it).
- **Strengths:** Prints gold. Buys anything — including other factions' heroes, whose defection triggers the defector's home faction to instantly declare war (this is the game's main diplomacy engine). €20 water concessions on their territory drain enemy armies passing through.
- **Weaknesses:** Weak native units (the crowd files a complaint instead of fighting). Universally hated: all other factions get a free "Business Techno!" war-cry buff when attacking them. Vulnerable to Authenticity damage they cannot heal, because they don't have the stat.
- **Rivalry lines:**
  - vs. Everyone: "Guys. Guys. There's a table for all of you. Minimum spend applies."
  - vs. Free Tek Convoy: "Love the aesthetic. Can we license it?"
  - vs. Door-State: "You reject 80% of people at the door? Interesting. We reject them at the price."

### FACTION 6: THE 15-SECOND SWARM *(TikTok Hard Techno)*
**"The drop is the whole song now."**

- **Personality:** The barbarian rush faction — the youngest tribe, moving at 155 BPM, wearing the fetish gear the old scenes invented and the sports sunglasses the old scenes banned, filming everything the old scenes made sacredly unfilmable. Derided by elders as "diet hardstyle" and "content." Genuinely does not care, which is their superpower and their whole military doctrine. Their morale bar is literally a follower count.
- **Aesthetic:** Mesh, harnesses, balaclavas (for the *camera*, which makes the 313 Foundation physically ill), ring lights mounted on siege engines, everything shot vertical.
- **Units:** *Shufflers* (fast infantry whose attack animation is a gloved routine; deal double damage while being filmed), *Edit Lords* (artillery that fires 15-second pop remixes; massive burst damage, zero sustain), *Ring Light Towers* (buff structures: everything in radius fights harder and looks better doing it), *The Girlfriend Of The DJ* (support unit, side-stage, mouthing "that's my boyfriend"; grants the hero unit +20% ego), *Viral Moments* (summonable events — one perfect clip spawns an instant army of new recruits from off-map).
- **Strengths:** Fastest expansion in the game; cheapest units; morale *grows* when elders publicly denounce them (every enemy "gatekeeping" action feeds their follower count). The 15-second drop beats the 8-minute build in any straight fight under 30 seconds.
- **Weaknesses:** Catastrophic attrition — no unit can sustain past the first drop; long games starve them. Morale collapses if engagement dips (a battle nobody films grants them nothing). Hardstyle mercenaries charge them triple, on principle, for "stealing our kicks and selling them to Berlin at 3x."
- **Rivalry lines:**
  - vs. The Foundry (industrial elders, see below): "OK grandpa, name three of MY tracks."
  - vs. Door-State: "You banned phones so we made the phone the venue."
  - vs. Rotterdam Legion: "We're literally the same!" — *(the Legion, in unison: "No.")*

### Non-Playable Tribes (neutral powers, mercenaries, and map creeps)
- **The Gains Kingdom (Hardstyle):** mercenary shock troops and the game's best pyrotechnics, hireable by anyone; shirtless, flag-caped, unironically sincere — the least cynical units in the game and immune to insult mechanics as a result. Their home map has a synchronized closing ceremony that all factions attend under truce.
- **The Cosmic Alliance (Psytrance):** neutral forest-and-sun civilization existing entirely outside the diplomacy graph — they cannot be insulted because they are not listening. Their chai tents are healing zones open to all factions; their decor crew outranks their DJ. Internally at war (Full-on vs. Forest goblins) in a conflict no one else can perceive.
- **The Order of the Echo (Dub Techno) & The Cult of the Patch Cable (Modular):** neutral observers at the back of every map, chins in hand, holding no territory anyone wants. The Order sells long-range fog-of-war tech (reverb literally obscures the map). The Cult never attacks anyone in recorded history; their win condition is completing their own tech tree, and every match they are present, they are quietly attempting it.
- **The New Beat Elders (Belgian EBM):** ancient creep camp guarding the 33 RPM Relic — an artifact that slows any unit, army, or entire faction to a narcotic crawl (+8). The hard counter to the Rotterdam Legion, discovered — per legend — by accident.
- **The Foundry (Industrial Old Guard):** a single grizzled hero-creep in a 20-year-old black t-shirt, frowning at a distortion pedal, tinnitus rendered as a permanent audio filter on his fog radius. Recruitable only by players whose Sell-Out Meter is at zero. Refuses to trend.

## 3. CORE RESOURCES & MECHANICS (techno concepts, not reskins)

### The Master Clock: BPM
There is no game speed slider; there is a **tempo fader**. Every production tick, attack cooldown, and movement rate is quantized to the current global BPM, which the player pitch-bends up and down like a DJ. High tempo = fast economy, fast combat, escalating **Burnout** drain. Low tempo = recovery, patience, minimal-purist bonuses. Faction identity is tempo identity: the Legion cannot function under 160; the Monks peak at 130; the 33 RPM Relic is a superweapon *because* it is slow. "When do I attack?" becomes "when do I push the tempo?" — and the soundtrack physically speeds up when you do.

### Primary Resource: **Vibe** (worker + mana in one crowd)
Your dancers ARE your economy. Crowds on your dancefloor generate Vibe while dancing; your abilities ("drops," auras, heals) *spend* Vibe from the same pool the same crowd regenerates. Overharvest and dancers leave the floor for the smoking area — visible, trackable, devastating. The eternal decision: **hype the floor or spend the floor.**

### Territory: **Bass Coverage** (sound systems as zone-of-control)
Speaker stacks project a literal audible radius = your territory (Company of Heroes point-capture model: no bass, no economy). Fog of war is **acoustic** — you *hear* enemy activity as muffled kick drums through the fog before you see anything; scouting means following the sound. Overlapping rival systems create **soundclash interference zones** where both incomes cancel until one rig wins or dies. Free Tek rigs are mobile; Door-State bass is a fortress; Franchise bass is licensed.

### Threat: **Heat** (the noise-complaint raid meter)
Every speaker upgrade, drop, and crowd-size threshold feeds a district Heat meter. Thresholds spawn noise inspectors (scouts) → patrols → a full **Raid** boss event on a countdown. Counterplay: soundproofing tech, bribing the council (Sell-Out Meter +1), relocating the entire party mid-match (base migration — the Free Tek specialty), or **Silent Disco mode** (Heat near zero, Vibe income halved, everyone looks ridiculous, the soundtrack cuts to just shuffling feet and one person going "woo"). Heat is the anti-turtle mechanic: the noise ordinance is the game's aggression tax.

### Corruption: **The Sell-Out Meter**
A Tropico-style temptation engine. Sponsorship gold, ghost producers, drink-price gouging, branded stages, playing the remix — all mechanically optimal, all ratcheting a meter that: repels purist units, cancels 313 Foundation diplomacy, spawns the war-cry "Business Techno!" buff for your enemies, and — at maximum — converts your faction cosmetically into The Franchise regardless of what you started as. The meter never decreases. The game keeps receipts.

### Defense: **Door Policy** (stance-based unit filtering)
Your chokepoints are doors, your towers are bouncers, and your defense tech tree is a **door-policy stance**: Strict (slow crowd income, filters out infiltrators — undercover officers, gatecrashers, journalists, stag parties) vs. Loose (fast income, high infiltration risk). The Door-State's oracle-tier doors reject on pure vibe with no stated criteria and no appeal; rejected units must leave silently — arguing triggers a permanent ban (unit deleted). Three questions and a vibe-scan; 80% rejection; working as intended.

### Intel: **The Camera Sticker** (fog of war as covenant)
Stickered territory cannot be photographed: no minimap detail, no replay footage, no screenshots — attempts produce a **pure black square** captioned with what you would have seen ("Trust us, it was incredible"). Enemy Content Team units caught filming inside stickered zones are confiscated. Posting the black square afterward grants a small Clout trophy — proof you were where no proof exists.

### Diplomacy: **The Smoking Area**
The only place in the game where units from hostile factions may stand together. The dancefloor is for communion; the smoking area is for parliament. All trades, truces, mercenary contracts, scene gossip (intel leaks), and betrayal set-ups occur here, conducted by delegates having the same five conversations forever ("is time real," "could you land a plane"). Attacking a smoking area is the game's one unforgivable war crime and unites the entire map against you.

### The Timer: **Sunrise**
Every match is one night. Victory is evaluated at dawn: cumulative Vibe, or survive-the-Raid, or hold-the-floor. The night has phases with rule changes — the 1 a.m. last-train crowd dip, the 3 a.m. peak, the afterhours license window — and a **golden-hour endgame** where all multipliers double and the shutters snap open to a roar. Klubnacht-doctrine factions (Door-State) can extend one night into three days, which is exactly as powerful and as costly as it sounds: **Afterhours Attrition** drains every surviving unit ("one more hour" may be declared 19 consecutive times), and Monday sunlight functions as a literal boss.

### The Superweapon: **The Drop**
A visible 32-bar riser synced to the actual soundtrack — an AoE2 Wonder you can *hear charging across the whole map*. Defend it while it builds; on release, mass buff + enemy stun + confetti. Enemies can **kill the drop**: cut the power, seize the mixer, or deploy the ultimate counter-unit — **The Purist**, who walks up to the booth mid-riser and says "actually, this is tech house," instantly deleting 8 bars of progress.

### Supply Cap: **Headroom** (the mix is the army)
Every unit and building adds a real stem to the live soundtrack; the mixer has finite channels. Your army composition is literally the arrangement of the track you're hearing — a Legion army sounds like war, a Monk army sounds like patience. Exceed headroom and the mix audibly clips, distorts, and trainwrecks: your crowd debuffs, your enemies hear it, and spectators laugh. The failure state is a bad DJ set, broadcast.

### Doctrine System: **Format Wars** (any faction may adopt one)
- **Vinyl Doctrine:** slower everything, +Prestige aura, units suffer Back Problems (30kg crate debuff), crate-digging unlocks Rare Record items.
- **USB Doctrine:** the pragmatic baseline; introduces **USB Anxiety** — your hero's entire ability loadout lives on one tiny losable object, and yes, there is a match event where it's in the other bag.
- **Sync Doctrine:** fastest production in the game; purist factions get a permanent diplomacy penalty against you and their units bark "we all hit play" sarcastically when yours die.
Historical footnote in the tooltip: every generation's cheating is the next generation's baseline.

## 5. MAPS & SETTINGS

- **MOTOR CITY (Foundation homeland):** Post-industrial cathedral map — silent auto plants as capturable production buildings that resurrect as studios; a pirate radio tower creep objective (*The Midnight Signal*) that broadcasts a map-wide buff to whoever holds it; an underwater Drexciyan approach lane only aquatic units can use.
- **THE POWERPLANT (Door-State homeland):** A vertical fortress map built inside a monumental power station — one door, 18-meter ceilings, a Panorama gallery whose shutters open at dawn for the golden-hour endgame. The queue outside is a physical lane where armies wait, silently, watching each other get rejected. There is a rumor of a fourth floor. There is no fourth floor. (There is a fourth floor.)
- **THE ORBITAL (UK free-party map):** A ring-road map where party sites spawn at random field-and-warehouse nodes revealed by treasure-hunt mechanics; police pressure sweeps the ring clockwise like weather; victory = keep a party alive somewhere, anywhere, until dawn. Common land in the center is unownable — a permanent free-fire festival zone with 23 capture points.
- **TWIN PORTS (Legion homeland):** Two rival harbor cities connected by one bridge. The map lies: the enemy city's nameplate just reads "AMSTERDAM?" and the mission objective is a question mark. Contains The Temple, a demolished mega-venue that exists only as a ghost-buff zone where Legion units gain +heart.
- **THE WHITE ISLE (Franchise homeland, "Babylon"):** Gold-economy paradise map — beach-club bases, sunset multipliers, a water-concession mechanic that drains visiting armies (€20/unit/minute), and the game's only map where the Sell-Out Meter *decreases*… no. Increases twice as fast. Sorry.
- **THE PARLIAMENT STEPS (protest-rave scenario):** The campaign's dramatic peak, played straight and with love: after a state raid on the drained-swimming-pool club under the stadium, every playable faction — Monks, Legion, Convoy, Swarm, even the Franchise (they brought the sound system, they'll invoice later) — deploys speakers-on-trucks in front of the seat of government for a survive-till-apology mission. Victory bark, all factions in unison: **"We dance together. We fight together."** This map has no jokes at the dancers' expense. Only at the ministers'.
- **THE SANDBOX AFTERS (post-match bonus map):** A riverside afterhours playground where the only way to tell time is by the quality of the free fruit being distributed. Survivors of the previous match wander in with their remaining units; there is no win condition; the fruit degrades; Monday approaches; someone has said "one more hour" nineteen times; the map ends when the last unit voluntarily goes home, which has never once happened in playtesting.

## 6. JOKE STYLE GUIDE

### Approved Formats (steal from the culture's own comedy engine)
1. **The Oracle's Verdict:** authority delivering absurd judgment with zero explanation. *"Heute leider nicht."* No appeal. Ever.
2. **Onion-style headline** (loading-screen news ticker): "Local Club Bans Smiling; Attendance Triples." "Selector's USB Found; Career Tragically Recovered." "Study Finds Lineup Doesn't Matter To Man Who Knows Entire Lineup."
3. **Types-of roster comedy:** every smoking area procedurally spawns The Careerist, The Dealer, The First Timer, The Philosopher ("is time real?" — in this game, canonically, no).
4. **The black square:** any forbidden screenshot renders as pure black + caption. ("You had to be there. You weren't.")
5. **Sacred-object comedy:** the USB, the carabiner (climbing nothing, ever), the coat-check tag, the camera sticker, the free fruit. Small things, liturgical gravity.
